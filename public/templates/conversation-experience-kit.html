Trust & Safety

Trust is built by being honest about uncertainty, recovering gracefully from errors, and never pretending to know more than the system knows.

Worked example · fictional company

NorthStar GmbH · Careers Assistant: a conversational front door for candidates

NorthStar hires food technologists, plant operators and office staff across its sites worldwide. The Careers Assistant answers candidates in chat and voice: finding roles, checking application status, scheduling interviews and handing over to a recruiter. Figures are illustrative.

Use it to

Make the system trustworthy without making it useless.

Time

45 minutes

Leads to

Agentic & Multimodal.

Principles

Confidence surfacing: high-confidence and low-confidence responses should look different

Design the "I don't know" response with as much care as the answer

Source attribution builds trust — anonymous information is unverifiable information

Safety guardrails should be designed to be invisible when not triggered, and clear when they are

Confidence surfacing

High confidence

State it plainly and act.

"Your interview is confirmed for Thursday 16 Oct, 10:00 CET."

Medium confidence

Show the reasoning and ask.

"I think you mean the Food Technologist role in Poznań. Is that right?"

Low confidence

Say so and route it.

"I am not sure about relocation support for that site. I have asked a recruiter, who replies within 2 working days."
"I don't know" response

Our version

What the system says when it cannot answer, and what it offers instead.

"I don't have that information. Here is who does: your recruiter, Anna. Want me to send her your question?"

What to do next

Repair success 71%

Most failed repairs are ambiguous dates. Replace open questions with a full-date confirmation and a date picker.

Trust score 6.8

Candidates who were escalated without a named recruiter scored lowest. Always name the person and the reply time.

# Case Study Writing Standard

> Reference: [Temi Adeniyi — Reimagining Onboarding for a Rapidly Evolving Product](https://www.temi-a.com/reimagining-onboarding-for-a-rapidly-evolving-product)
>
> This page is the quality bar. "The level of balance between the macro and micro level of detailing is needed in these case studies as a design leader who is also a hands-on expert and have craft as the soul."

---

## The Core Principle

Every case study must operate in two simultaneous registers:

- **Macro** — org constraints, strategic reframes, systemic decisions, business context
- **Micro** — named principles, specific examples, metrics with explanatory sentences, real frameworks

Neither alone is sufficient. A case study that's only strategic sounds like a consultant. One that's only craft-detailed sounds like a practitioner who doesn't see the full picture. The balance is what marks a design leader with craft as the soul.

---

## Structural Formula

| Section | What it does | What it is NOT |
|---|---|---|
| Business situation | Sets the org/product context and stakes | A design brief or project overview |
| The problem | Names the challenge at 3 levels: user, product, org | "Users needed a better experience" |
| Strategy | Named as a philosophy or approach | A list of deliverables or methods |
| Leadership moves | Numbered decisions, each with a specific action and result | A process walkthrough |
| Outcomes | Metrics with context — the resistance overcome + what it unlocked | Raw numbers |
| Why it mattered | Synthesis of broader significance | A summary of what was built |

---

## Macro Moves — Strategic Register

- **Open with business/product evolution**, not a design problem. Stakes first.
- **Name org dysfunction explicitly** — missing roles, fragmented teams, devolved roadmaps. The constraint most designers wouldn't mention.
  - Example: *"Growth had no product lead. Activation had no senior PM. The roadmap had devolved into disconnected experiments."*
- **Show scope expansion as a strategic decision**: "this started as X; I reframed it as Y"
- **Show systemic decisions**: creating new roles, establishing ways of working, shifting team culture
- **Name the tension** explicitly: *"This surfaced the core tension: how do we honour X while doing Y?"*
- **Org influence as outcome**: "strengthened alignment between teams", "established a repeatable approach", "replaced opinion-driven decisions with principle-led ones"

---

## Micro Moves — Craft Register

- **Name the exact principles**, not just "we established principles"
  - Good: *Make it personal / Highlight shopping / Connect the dots*
  - Bad: *"We defined three onboarding principles"*

- **Name segments with behavioral specifics and data**
  - Good: *"Tracking-first buyers (majority) — 70% downloaded Shop via an order confirmation button"*
  - Bad: *"We identified three user segments"*

- **Show the vision→hypothesis→experiment chain as a live example**:
  ```
  Vision principle: Highlight shopping
        ↓
  Central hypothesis: Help buyers quickly understand Shop's value and remove barriers to starting
        ↓
  Experiment: A one-screen onboarding flow that communicates shopping messaging more clearly
  ```
  Not as a diagram — as real, named content.

- **Every metric needs its explanatory sentence**:
  - Not: `+9% email verification`
  - Yes: `+9% email verification — connecting Gmail had high friction due to trust concerns, but once users verified, they had significantly higher retention rates`

## Voice Rules

| Write this | Not this |
|---|---|
| "I reframed the work as..." | "The team explored..." |
| "I advocated for a dedicated designer..." | "We recognised the need for..." |
| "I aligned with peers to expand scope" | "Scope was expanded to include..." |
| "This surfaced the core tension" | "We identified a challenge" |
| "The framework replaced the unfocused roadmap" | "This helped improve alignment" |

Active, first-person, specific. Every sentence should show the decision being made, not the process that led to it.

---

## What Doesn't Belong

- Deliverable-first language: *"I designed a carousel / a flow / a component"*
- Process-as-filler: *discovery → ideation → prototyping → testing*
- Generic outcomes: *"improved user experience", "increased engagement", "enhanced usability"*
- Passive attribution: *"design decisions were informed by research"*
- Screenshots as story: screens are contextual evidence, not the narrative

---

## Application Checklist for Amit's Case Studies

For each case study in `work.ts`, verify:

- [ ] Opens with business/product context — not a brief
- [ ] Names at least one org/structural constraint explicitly
- [ ] Strategy is framed as a philosophy, not a deliverable list
- [ ] Has at least 3 named leadership moves (numbered, decision-forward)
- [ ] Shows a principle-→-hypothesis-→-experiment chain with real content
- [ ] Every stat has an explanatory sentence (resistance overcome + what it unlocked)
- [ ] Voice is first-person, active, ownership language
- [ ] No process-as-filler sections
- [ ] Screens/visuals are positioned as evidence for a claim, not as the claim
